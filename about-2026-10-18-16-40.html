ℹ️ About the Society

The San Francisco Ladies’ Protection and Relief Society was founded on August 4, 1853, by Mrs. A. B. Eaton, to help women and children left stranded by the Gold Rush. More than 170 years later it is still running, now as Heritage on the Marina, a nonprofit continuing care retirement community in San Francisco’s Marina District offering independent living, assisted living, and memory care.

Over its long life the Society has sheltered women, raised children, and cared for older San Franciscans. Its home on Laguna Street was designed by Julia Morgan, with later additions by Warren Charles Perry and Gardner Dailey. The building was named a San Francisco Designated Landmark in 2024.

Founded: August 4, 1853, by Mrs. A. B. Eaton

Today: Heritage on the Marina, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit

Location: Laguna Street, Marina District, San Francisco

Website: heritageonthemarina.org

🗓️ Timeline

1853
Society founded to aid women and families affected by the Gold Rush.
1857
Opens “Hospitality House,” a shelter for women and children.
1860–1863
Horace Hawes gives a lot on Franklin Street; the children’s home there becomes known as the “Old Brown Ark.”
1906
After the earthquake the Ark is declared unsafe, and the children move to tents in the Presidio.
1922
Focus shifts from children to elderly and convalescent women; the Laguna Street site is acquired.
1925
Julia Morgan’s Laguna Street building opens on March 3.
1953
Centennial celebration.
1950s
Merger with the Crocker Old People’s Home; its residents move to Laguna Street (1957).
1958–1959
The Perry building is completed, and the enlarged home is named “The Heritage.”
1963
The Health Center, designed by Gardner Dailey, is built.
1977
Chinatown Kitchen Programs begin, serving low-income seniors.
1989
The Heritage comes through the Loma Prieta earthquake almost undamaged.
2000
The Trustees and the Board of Managers merge into one Board of Directors.
2003
150th anniversary.
2024
Named a San Francisco Designated Landmark.

Sources disagree on the date of the Crocker merger (1954, 1955, and 1957 all appear).

🗂️ About this archive

This site holds photographs of the Society’s minute books and records from 1906 to 2020: 3,468 pages in 25 volumes, from the Board of Trustees, the Board of (Lady) Managers, the Crocker Old People’s Home, the modern Board of Directors, and a few commemorative items such as the 1953 centennial guest book. The pages were photographed in 2025 and organized into this archive in 2026.

You can browse by volume or search the text of every page. The text was read by machine (OCR), so it contains errors, and handwritten pages are not searchable.
